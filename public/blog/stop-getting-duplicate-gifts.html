5 Ways to Stop Getting Duplicate Gifts on Your Birthday

By WishNest • 4 min read

You’re opening presents at your birthday dinner. Your friend hands you a bag, you pull out the exact same bestselling novel your aunt gave you ten minutes ago. You force a smile and say, "Wow, I've really been wanting this!" Technically, it's true, but now you have to figure out how to discretely return one of them.

If you're wondering how to avoid getting duplicate gifts on your birthday, you are not alone. It’s incredibly common, especially when you have distinct hobbies that everyone knows about. Here are five foolproof ways to prevent it.

1. Use a Tool with a "Reserve" Feature

The number one reason people buy the same gift is that they don't talk to each other. The easiest fix is a digital wishlist that allows friends to anonymously "claim" or reserve a gift. When someone reserves an item, it is marked off for everyone else, but remains a surprise to you. Creating a wishlist with WishNest does exactly this.

2. Broaden Your "Small Gifts" Horizon

People often default to buying candles, socks, or mugs because they are safe, affordable bets. To avoid getting four different vanilla candles, populate your list with highly specific small items. Think about niche sauces, a specific coffee blend from a local roaster, or a peculiar board game.

3. Suggest Consumables

If you genuinely don't mind duplicates, steer people toward things you can consume. If you get three bottles of your favorite wine or four boxes of premium tea, it's not a problem! You will eventually use them all. Just be sure to specify which consumables you prefer.

4. The "One Big Thing" Strategy

Instead of asking for ten small things, tell your close circle that you are saving up for one major purchase—like a new mattress, a weekend getaway, or a gaming console. Friends and family can pool their resources to get you something truly meaningful, entirely eliminating the risk of duplicates.

5. Delegate a "Gift Coordinator"

If you are throwing a large party and don't want to manage a registry yourself, ask a partner or best friend to be the point of contact. Put a tiny note on the invitation: "No gifts expected! But if you’d like to bring something, text Sarah to coordinate." Sarah can then make sure nobody overlaps.
